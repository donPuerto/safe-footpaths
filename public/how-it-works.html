What it is

A two-week work plan for two footpath-repair crews (Crew A and Crew B). The backlog of jobs, the boss's priority rules and the crews' settings live in a Google Sheet. An n8n workflow (the planner) reads the Sheet, ranks the jobs, fills each work day crew by crew, and writes the result back to the Sheet. This page shows that result as a calendar and lets the scheduler (Dan) move, resize, reorder or lock jobs. Every change is saved to the Sheet and the planner re-runs by itself.

The rules

Jobs are compared rule by rule, in the order set in the Rules tab. The first rule where two jobs differ decides which comes first; the oldest approval date is the tie-breaker. In simple words:

  1. School and university jobs go into their holidays (none fall in this window, so this rule is quiet).
  2. Anything the director marked as priority comes next.
  3. Jobs already in progress (not council) are finished before new ones are started.
  4. If a short job (under a day) is within about 30 minutes' drive of a job already on that day, do it the same day.
  5. Rework, then jobs the client calls urgent, then jobs the client is chasing.
  6. Other private jobs, then council jobs in progress, then new council jobs.

Every card shows which rule placed it, for example Rank 2 â€“ Rule 2: Director Priority, so the order is always explainable.

What Dan can do on the page

  • Move: drag a card to another day or crew. A red lane means it does not fit; the toast says why.
  • Resize: drag the right edge to make a job 1, 2 or more days. Dan's length replaces the estimate for that job only.
  • Order: drag a card up or down within a day. The number in the circle is the crew's order that day.
  • Lock / Unlock: open a card and choose.
  • Place it: on the Needs a human tab, book a job the planner refused to place.

What the AI does, and does not do

There is one AI step in the planner. It reads each job's free-text notes (inspector notes, scheduling notes, client contact) and returns facts only: a date window, "urgent", "chasing", rework, a notice period, a blocker. Every fact must come with a word-for-word quote from the notes.

The AI never ranks, never places a job, and never decides what goes to Needs a human. Code does all of that. Think of it as an assistant who reads every email and highlights the important sentences; the checklist (code) verifies each highlight and applies the rules; the boss (Dan) decides the cases the checklist cannot.

Guardrails when the AI is wrong

Five layers. Each catches what the one before it misses.

  1. The AI is never allowed to decide. It only reads notes and returns facts. A bad answer cannot book a job on its own.
  2. Every fact needs a word-for-word quote. Code checks the quote really appears in the job's notes. A date is accepted only if its month is named in the quote. This stops invented facts.
  3. Keyword cross-checks catch what the AI left out. If the notes say "urgent", a month name or "school holidays" and the AI returned nothing for it, the job fails. This stops omitted facts. It was built after a live run where the AI skipped three date windows.
  4. A failed check sends the job to Needs a human, not the calendar. A person decides, with the reason in front of them.
  5. The Checks tab is the last net. Twelve checks run on the finished plan, including "jobs that cannot be placed are not in the plan". If any fails, the plan is not written, the last good plan stays on this page and an alert email goes out.

The code behind Needs a human

No AI here. One planner step runs a short list of if … then … rules on every job. Here they are, in plain words and in the order they run.

1. Is the AI's reading of the notes trustworthy?

The AI's answer is for a different job            -> not trusted
A fact has no quote, or the quote is not in the notes -> not trusted
The notes say "urgent", a month, or "school holidays"
   but the AI said nothing about it                 -> not trusted

A job that is "not trusted" goes to the tray as Not placed · AI check failed. The AI's mistake never reaches the calendar.

2. Can this job be done by our crews in these two weeks?

The job is not in Victoria                        -> Not placed
Someone must approve or plan it first             -> Not placed
The estimate or duration can't be read            -> Not placed
It needs more than 4 technicians                  -> Not placed
The client's dates are before or after the window -> Not placed

3. Book the jobs

Dan's moves and locks go in first, exactly where he put them.
   (If his choice is a holiday, a full day, or one crew for a 4-person job
    -> "Dan's change can't be kept", and he picks again.)
Then each day is filled, best-ranked job first, until it is full.

4. Look at what is left

A good job that found no free days              -> No room in window
A crew's day has more than 3 hours of driving    -> Check driving (the jobs stay booked)

That is the whole evaluation. Eleven made-up jobs, one per rule above, are run through this code by a test (node engine/test-eligibility.js) to prove each lands in the right place.

Changing a rule, no rewiring

  1. Open the Rules tab in the Sheet.
  2. Change a rule's order number (decimals are fine: 2.5 puts it between 2 and 3), or set enabled to FALSE to switch it off.
  3. Run the planner (or save any change on this page) and the new order applies. Every card's reason updates with it.

Holidays, the plan window, the crews and the drive limit are in the Settings tab in the same way.

Nothing on this page writes to the Sheet. Saving a change on the plan page asks for the shared passcode.
